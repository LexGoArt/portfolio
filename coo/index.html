B · Method

Seven rules I apply from week one

They are boring on purpose. An operation that depends on someone being clever every morning is not an operation, it is a rescue.

  1. B.01

    Map first, decide second

    Discovery before build: interviews, customer journeys, competitor checks. Most of the savings come from work that never starts.

  2. B.02

    One source of truth

    A dozen spreadsheets that disagree is not data, it is a negotiation.

  3. B.03

    Roles, not heroes

    Governance framework, written role and process documentation, reporting standards. The system has to survive the person who built it leaving.

  4. B.04

    Priority is calculated, not argued

    MoSCoW and WSJF on the backlog. When the ranking is arithmetic, the loudest voice stops setting the roadmap.

  5. B.05

    Risks are named before they bite

    RAID logs kept live, not reconstructed afterwards. A risk written down is a decision waiting; a risk unwritten is a surprise with a date on it.

  6. B.06

    AI in the process, not in the deck

    I do not run pilots. I take a recurring task, measure what it costs today, rebuild it, and keep it only if the number moves.

  7. B.07

    Reporting a board reads in two minutes

    A KPI matrix, not a deck. If an owner needs a meeting to understand status, the reporting failed, not the owner.

C.01 / Operating model / 2026 / Iron Invest Group

Problem
Two holdings, 27 active projects, €15M+ in construction budgets, and every project run its own way. No common governance, no shared reporting, no written process.
Action
Built the operating model from the ground up: governance framework, role and process documentation, reporting standards. Then stood up a project office to hold the standard across all 27.
Result
One delivery standard across both offices, 60 in-house staff and the contractor network working to the same definitions.

C.02 / Internal product / 2026 / Dobrograd

Problem
Unit availability, pricing and reservations lived in parallel files. Sales quoted from whichever copy they had open.
Action
Specified and shipped one live registry: search, map, price list, role-based access, instalment calculator.
Result
2,121 units across nine developments in one place, visible to the sales floor and to management in the same second.

C.03 / AI in operations / 2026 / Iron Invest Group

Problem
"AI saves time" is easy to claim and impossible to budget against. I wanted a number an owner could argue with.
Action
Took 32 delivered pieces of work and priced each one four ways: with AI, by me without AI, by a remote specialist, by a specialist in the office.
Result
×13 cheaper and ×10 faster on the measured set, published in full with the method and the caveats visible.

D

D · Sequence

My first 90 days

The same sequence every time. I change nothing in month one, because the first thing an operation needs is an honest picture of itself.

Days 1–30

Look, do not touch

  • Map every process, project and reporting line as they actually run
  • Interview the people doing the work, not only those describing it
  • Find where money and time leak, with numbers attached

You getAn honest map and a ranked list of leaks.

Days 31–60

Fix the loudest leak

  • Close the single most expensive gap, visibly, so the change is credible
  • Stand up one source of truth for whatever people argue about most
  • Install a reporting cadence the owner reads in two minutes

You getOne measurable win and reporting you can trust.

Days 61–90

Make it hold without me

  • Write the governance: roles, processes, delivery standard
  • Automate the repetitive parts and measure what that saved
  • Hand over dashboards and the operating rhythm to the team

You getA system that runs when I am on holiday.
